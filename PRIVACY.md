# Privacy

HeyNYC answers questions about NYC services over SMS and WhatsApp. Here is what happens to your messages, stated plainly. This is the short version; the formal notice, including service providers, children's privacy, and email contacts, is the [HeyNYC Privacy Notice](docs/legal/HEYNYC-PRIVACY.md), which controls if the two ever disagree.

**What we process.** Your messages are carried by the SMS/WhatsApp network (Twilio) like any text you send, and the conversation context needed for a reply is sent to our configured AI model provider to generate the answer. The current safety screen may send the current message and one preceding resident message to the configured safety model before the answer is generated. That processing is what makes the service work; no service like this can truthfully promise your words touch no infrastructure.

**What we keep.** An encrypted conversation transcript and any in-progress application draft, for the configured retention period, on our infrastructure. Twilio messages also enter an encrypted inbox before the webhook acknowledges them, so a restart does not silently lose the request; their queued content is scrubbed after the reply is accepted or removed when retention expires. The single-host deployment path creates snapshots containing those encrypted records and removes expired snapshots on the same configured schedule. We don't build a profile on you, and the eligibility-screening flow is PII-free by design. Don't paste an SSN or similar sensitive ID into the chat; the assistant will tell you the same.

**Who reads it.** No person reads conversations in the normal course of operating the service. Someone who runs HeyNYC decrypts a real resident's conversation in exactly two cases: you sent an exchange to us with REPORT and confirmed, which shares only that one exchange; or a specific safety or abuse review requires it. Debugging and development use the operators' own test conversations, never yours, and curiosity is not a case. We state this as policy rather than impossibility, because the operator of any encrypted service holds its keys, and we would rather be straight about that.

**Your controls.** NEW starts a fresh conversation the assistant no longer sees earlier messages of (the audit record is retained). PRIVACY returns this policy in short form. REPORT flags your last exchange for human review, with your confirmation. DELETE MY DATA erases your live data: after you confirm, it first prevents older deployment snapshots from being restored, then deletes your encrypted conversation transcript, queued messages, any in-progress application draft, and pending report flags. Expired snapshots are removed on the configured retention schedule. What survives is only PII-free aggregate service statistics and an anonymized daily spend record kept for abuse control, neither of which identifies you. The service cannot undo a confirmed deletion.

**Access and portability.** You can request a copy of information held directly by Reach4Help at [privacy@reach4help.org](mailto:privacy@reach4help.org). A self-service export is planned but not shipped. The planned archive will include a human-readable conversation copy and a versioned JSON copy, an [IETF-standard data-interchange format](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8259/), after the requester controls the same messaging address and confirms the request. Verification through the account's existing email address or phone number and a time-limited download are established chat-export practices ([OpenAI data export](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7260999-how-do-i-export-my-chatgpt-history-and-data)). This follows the principle that portable data should be structured, commonly used, and machine-readable in [GDPR Article 20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/art_20/oj); it is a design reference, not a claim that HeyNYC has been certified or independently audited for compliance.

**Moving or recovering the service.** The internal database is not a resident export format. The [single-host deployment path](scripts/deploy_wsl.sh) creates and verifies a consistent snapshot of encrypted resident records before switching releases. It rejects expired snapshots and any snapshot from before a confirmed deletion. Snapshot expiry uses systemd's [native temporary-file cleanup](https://www.freedesktop.org/software/systemd/man/systemd-tmpfiles.html). A future host migration still needs a supervised transfer of the snapshot, encryption key, and pseudonymization salt before cutover. SQLite documents both its [cross-platform file format](https://www.sqlite.org/onefile.html) and its [safe online backup mechanism](https://sqlite.org/backup.html); NIST contingency guidance calls for protecting backups and testing restoration, not merely creating copies ([NIST SP 800-34](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/34/r1/upd1/final)).

**Spend and abuse limits.** Per-person daily usage caps protect the service without cutting anyone off from the deterministic emergency guidance, which always works.
